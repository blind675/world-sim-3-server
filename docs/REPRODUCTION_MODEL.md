# Reproduction and inheritance

Milestone 9 adds a bounded population loop for the existing fictional ground bird. The checked-in [reproduction policy](../config/reproduction-policy.json) defines a 60-agent default cap, a 30-day minimum breeding age, a 20-tick pairing check, a 64-cell partner radius, a seven-day cooldown, parental hunger/thirst limits, parental needs costs, and a small mutation range for each numeric inherited trait. Milestone 10 widened the radius from 20 to 64 cells after a three-seed 31-day run produced no births at 20 cells and two seeds produced births at 64. The age threshold is at least the adult stage in the [agent template](../config/agent-template.json). If a world was configured with more than 60 initial agents, its cap remains that configured starting count; reproduction cannot increase it further.

At each pairing check, the server examines living eligible females in stable spawn order and chooses the nearest eligible male within the wrapped-world radius. A parent can be used once per check. Birth requires a free, walkable neighbor cell beside the mother. An unavailable cell, poor hunger or thirst, age below the minimum, cooldown, or a full population blocks birth without charging either parent. Successful births increase each parent's hunger by 0.08 and thirst by 0.04, which creates a bounded extra food and water demand. The costs are applied once after placing the newborn. The child joins after the adult update loop and begins its own needs/perception update on the next tick.

Numeric inherited traits start at the parents' midpoint, receive a separately bounded mutation, and are clamped to the template's bounds. Vision range, memory capacity, and lifespan are whole numbers. Circadian type comes from one parent and sex is seeded. The existing walking `moveSpeed` trait now scales the child's route cells per two-tick movement segment; founding agents retain their speed of 1 and previous movement. A newborn has an empty path, inventory, memory, and behavior state, with zero initial hunger, thirst, and tiredness. `parentIds` record mother then father. Parents save `lastBirthTick` for cooldown.

The snapshot saves a separate birth PRNG state, cumulative `birthCount`, and monotonic `nextBirthId`; new births receive IDs `a-51`, `a-52`, and so on in a default 50-agent world. IDs are never reused when a child dies. The snapshot also pins the reproduction policy's hash and validates population fields before readiness. A milestone 8 snapshot with no policy hash gains empty parent links, no prior births, and a counter above the known initial and surviving/dead IDs. Milestone 10 accepts the **exact** built-in milestone 9 policy hash under revision 2. The wider search changes future pairing decisions but does not reset agents, needs, positions, or counters. Custom mismatched policies still fail startup clearly. The next save pins revision 2; back up the persistent volume before deploying it. `GET /api/reproduction-policy`, the `birthCount` and `populationCap` status fields, and `parentIds` in agent responses are read-only; public viewers have no breeding control.

Births do not require an extra server process, database, or live editor. The hard cap bounds memory and snapshot growth, but a 60-agent world may use more memory than the 50-agent baseline. The [milestone 9 reproduction benchmark](baselines/reproduction-m9.md) exercises a synthetic mature population and reports births, resource demand, tick cost, and snapshot growth, including a 2,500-tick run near the cap. It skips the first 30 days of ordinary survival and therefore does not predict stable population or extinction. With the 20-second production tick, adulthood begins after roughly 60 hours of uninterrupted simulation if agents survive. The [milestone 10 population report](baselines/population-m10.md) measures natural births and age deaths separately.
